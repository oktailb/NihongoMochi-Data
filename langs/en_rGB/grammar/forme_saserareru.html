The Causative-Passive Form (使役受身形しえきうけみけい)

The Causative-Passive Form is used to express that you were made to do or forced to do something against your will. It combines the Causative (making someone do) and the Passive (having something done to you).

1. How to Conjugate

This form can be quite long. For Group 1 verbs, there is a common shortened version.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound to the "a" sound and add せられる (Long) or される (Short).

  • 書かく → 書かかせられる / 書かかされる (forced to write)
  • 読よむ → 読よませられる / 読よまされる (forced to read)
  • 待まつ → 待またせられる / 待またされる (forced to wait)

⚠️ Note: For verbs ending in す (like 話はなす), only the long form させられる is used.

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Remove the final る and add させられる.

  • 食たべる → 食たべさせられる (forced to eat)
  • 見みる → 見みさせられる (forced to watch)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する → させられる (forced to do)
  • 来くる → 来こさせられる (forced to come)

2. Sentence Structure

The person who forces you to do the action is marked with the particle に.

[Person Forced] は [Person Forcing] に [Verb Causative-Passive]。

  • 私わたしは母ははに部屋へやを掃除そうじさせられました。
    (I was forced by my mother to clean the room.)
  • 子供こどもの時とき、嫌いな野菜やさいを食たべさせられました。
    (When I was a child, I was made to eat vegetables I disliked.)

3. Why use it?

In Japanese culture, expressing that you did something because you were forced to (rather than by choice) is important for social context. It shows that you are not responsible for the action or that you are inconvenienced by it.
