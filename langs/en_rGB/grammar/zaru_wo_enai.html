"Cannot help but...": ～ざるを得ない

The expression ～ざるを得ない (～ざるをえない) is a formal way to say that you have no choice but to do something, even if you don't want to. It implies that external circumstances or logic force your hand.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach ざるを得ない to the Nai-form stem of a verb (remove the ~nai).

[Verb Nai-stem] + ざるを得ない

  • 嫌きらいな仕事しごとだが、生活せいかつのために続つづけざるを得ない。
    (It's a job I hate, but for my livelihood, I have no choice but to continue.)
  • これだけの証拠しょうこがあれば、かれが犯人はんにんだと認みとめざるを得ない。
    (With this much evidence, I cannot help but admit that he is the criminal.)

⚠️ Irregular: The verb する becomes せざるを得ない.

2. Key Nuance: Formal & Objective

Unlike ~shikanai (casual), this is very formal and often used in writing or official statements. It suggests that you are making a logical conclusion or following a strong necessity.

3. Distinction from ～しかない

  • ～しかない: Casual/Standard. Often personal feelings ("I just have to do it!").
  • ～ざるを得ない: Formal/Official. Focuses on unavoidable necessity or logic.
