The Passive Form (受身形うけみけい)

The Passive Form is used when an action is performed on the subject. In Japanese, it is very common when expressing that something was done to you, often with a nuance of being troubled or affected by it.

1. How to Conjugate

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound to the "a" sound and add れる.

  • 書かく (to write) → 書かかれる (to be written)
  • 踏ふむ (to step on) → 踏ふまれる (to be stepped on)
  • 呼よぶ (to call) → 呼よばれる (to be called)

⚠️ Exception: If it ends in う, it becomes われる (e.g., 言いう → 言いわれる).

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Remove the final る and add られる.

  • 食たべる → 食たべられる
  • 褒ほめる (to praise) → 褒ほめられる

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する → される
  • 来くる → 来こられる (pronounced ko-rareru)

2. Sentence Structure

The person doing the action is marked with the particle に.

[Victim] は [Doer] に [Verb Passive Form]。

  • 私わたしは先生せんせいに褒ほめられました。
    (I was praised by the teacher.)
  • 泥棒どろぼうに財布さいふを盗ぬすまれました。
    (My wallet was stolen by a thief / I had my wallet stolen by a thief.)

3. The "Suffering" Passive

In Japanese, the passive is often used to show that you were inconvenienced by someone else's action.

  • 雨あめに降ふられました。
    (It rained on me / I was troubled by the rain falling.)
