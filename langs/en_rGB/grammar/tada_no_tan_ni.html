Simply and Just: ただの / 単に (たんに)

When you want to strip away complexity and say that something is "just" X and nothing more, we use ただの or 単に. They are the ultimate tools for simplicity and downplaying significance.

1. Sentence Structure

  • ただの + Noun
  • 単に + [Verb/Adj/Noun] + だけだ

2. Simple Examples

  • これはただの冗談じょうだんです。
    (This is just a joke.)
  • 彼かれは単に忘わすれただけだ。
    (He simply forgot, that's all.)
  • 単に面白おもしろいからやっているにすぎない。
    (I'm doing it simply because it's interesting, and for no other reason.)

3. Sensei's Note

ただの is like "merely" or "ordinary." 単に is more like "solely" or "purely." Both help you isolate a single, simple reason or identity. Use them when someone is over-analyzing a situation and you want to bring them back to the basics!
