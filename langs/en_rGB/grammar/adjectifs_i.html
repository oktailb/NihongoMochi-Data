I-Adjectives (い形容詞いけいようし)

In Japanese, adjectives are divided into two groups. I-adjectives are unique because they can be conjugated just like verbs to show tense (past, negative, etc.).

1. Identifying I-Adjectives

All I-adjectives end with the hiragana い. (Note: A few Na-adjectives like きれい also end in 'i', so be careful!)

  • 大おおきい (Big)
  • 小ちいさい (Small)
  • 高たかい (Expensive / Tall)
  • 暑あつい (Hot)
  • おいしい (Delicious)

2. Conjugation Rules

To change the tense, remove the final い and add the following endings:

Tense Ending Example (高たかい)
Present Affirmative ～い 高たかい (It is expensive)
Present Negative ～くない 高たかくない (It is not expensive)
Past Affirmative ～かった 高たかかった (It was expensive)
Past Negative ～くなかった 高たかくなかった (It was not expensive)

3. The Special Case: いい (Good)

The word for "good" is irregular. Its conjugations are based on its original form: 良よい.

  • Present Affirmative: いい
  • Present Negative: 良よくない
  • Past Affirmative: 良よかった
  • Past Negative: 良よくなかった

4. Usage

You can use them directly before a noun or at the end of a sentence.

  • 高たかい車くるま
    (An expensive car)
  • この車くるまは高たかいです。
    (This car is expensive.)
