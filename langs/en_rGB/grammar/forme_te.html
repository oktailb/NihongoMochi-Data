The Te-form (て形てけい) - The "Swiss Army Knife" of Japanese

The te-form is a versatile verb form used to connect sentences, describe ongoing actions, or make requests. It doesn't have a tense of its own; it inherits the tense of the final verb in the sentence.

1. How to Conjugate

Formation depends on which group the verb belongs to. Mastering these rules is essential for your progress!

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

The ending of the dictionary form determines the change:

Ending Change to... Example (辞書形じしょけい → て形てけい)
う, つ, る ～って 買かう → 買かって
む, ぶ, ぬ ～んで 読よむ → 読よんで
く ～いて 書かく → 書かいて
ぐ ～いで 泳およぐ → 泳およいで
す ～して 話はなす → 話はなして

⚠️ Exception: The verb 行いく becomes 行いって.

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

These are the easiest! Just drop the final る and add て.

  • 食たべる → 食たべて
  • 見みる → 見みて
  • 寝ねる → 寝ねて

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

There are only two, so simply memorize them:

  • する → して
  • 来くる → 来きて

2. Why is it useful?

Once you know the te-form, you can build many useful expressions:

  1. Connecting Actions: Listing things you do in order.
    朝起あさおきて、コーヒーを飲のみます。
  2. Requests (～てください): Asking someone to do something.
    日本語にほんごで話はなしてください。
  3. Ongoing Actions (～ている): Describing what is happening right now.
    今いま、勉強べんきょうしています。
  4. Sequence (～てから): Saying "After doing X, I do Y".
    手てを洗あらってから、食たべます。
