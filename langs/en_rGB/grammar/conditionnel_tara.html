The Versatile Conditional: ～たら

The ～たら conditional is the most common and versatile way to say "if" or "when" in Japanese. It is used when one action happens after another is completed.

1. How to Conjugate

Simply take the た形たけい (Informal Past) and add ら.

Verbs

  • 行いく → 行いった → 行いったら (If/When I go)
  • 食たべる → 食たべた → 食たべたら (If/When I eat)

Adjectives and Nouns

  • い-Adjectives: Replace the final ～い with ～かったら.
    安やすい → 安やすかったら (If it is cheap)
  • な-Adjectives / Nouns: Add ～だったら.
    暇ひま → 暇ひまだったら (If you are free)
    雨あめ → 雨あめだったら (If it is rain)

2. When to use it?

  1. Hypothetical "If": When something might happen.
    お金があったら、車くるまを買かいます。
    (If I had money, I would buy a car.)
  2. Temporal "When": When an action is certain to happen in the future.
    家うちに着ついたら、電話でんわしてください。
    (When you get home, please call me.)

3. The "Golden Rule"

Unlike ～と, you can use commands, requests, and opinions in the second part of a ～たら sentence. It is the safest "if" to use in daily conversation!
