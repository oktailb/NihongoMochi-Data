While you're at it: ～ついでに

The expression ～ついでに is used when you perform a second action while you are already doing a primary action. It translates to "while," "as long as," or "incidentally."

1. Sentence Structure

Attach ついでに to the Dictionary Form or Past Form (Ta-form) of a verb, or to a Noun (using の).

  • 散歩さんぽするついでに、はがきを出だしてきました。
    (While taking a walk, I [incidentally] mailed a postcard.)
  • スーパーへ行いったついでに、牛乳ぎゅうにゅうを買かいました。
    (While I was at the supermarket, I bought some milk.)
  • 用事ようじのついでに、図書館としょかんによってきました。
    (Since I had errands to run, I stopped by the library.)

2. Key Nuance: Opportunity

Unlike ～ながら (which focuses on simultaneous actions), ～ついでに implies taking advantage of an existing opportunity to do something else that was not the main purpose.
