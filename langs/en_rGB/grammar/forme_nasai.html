Giving Commands: ～なさい

The ～なさい form is used to give a firm command or instruction. While it is grammatically polite (derived from the honorific nasaru), it carries a strong tone of authority. It is typically used by parents to children, teachers to students, or in written exam instructions.

1. How to Conjugate

Attach なさい directly to the Verb Stem (the part before ~masu).

[Verb Stem] + なさい

  • 食たべます → 食たべ + なさい = 食たべなさい (Eat!)
  • 行いきます → 行いき + なさい = 行いきなさい (Go!)
  • します → し + なさい = しなさい (Do it!)

2. Key Examples

  • 「もう遅おそいから、早はやく寝ねなさい。」
    ("It's already late, so go to sleep quickly.")
  • 「テレビを消けして、宿題しゅくだいをしなさい。」
    ("Turn off the TV and do your homework.")
  • 「つぎの質問しつもんに答こたえなさい。」
    ("Answer the following question.")

3. Nuance: When NOT to use it

Because ～なさい implies a clear hierarchy where the speaker has authority over the listener, you should never use it toward a superior (boss, teacher) or a stranger. In those cases, use ～てください for requests.

4. Quick Tip: ～なさい vs ～ろ (Imperative)

  • ～なさい: Firm and authoritative, but used in a "corrective" or educational way (parents, teachers).
  • ～ろ (Imperative form): Very rough, aggressive, or used in emergencies. It sounds much harsher than ~nasai.
