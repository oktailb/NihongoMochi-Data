Just Because X Doesn't Mean Y: ～からといって

We often make assumptions based on one fact. ～からといって is used to challenge those assumptions! It means "Even though it is true that X, it doesn't necessarily follow that Y." It's usually followed by a negative like wake dewa nai or kagiranai.

1. Sentence Structure

[Plain Form] + からといって + [Negative Conclusion]

2. Logical Examples

  • 日本にほんに住すんでいるからといって、日本語にほんごがペラペラになるとは限かぎらない。
    (Just because you live in Japan, it doesn't mean you'll automatically become fluent in Japanese.)
  • 高たかいからといって、いいものだとは言いえない。
    (Just because it's expensive, you can't say it's a good thing.)
  • 嫌きらいだからといって、何なにも食たべないのはよくない。
    (Just because you dislike it, it's not good to eat nothing at all.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a great tool for critical thinking! It warns the listener not to jump to conclusions. It acknowledges the first part as a fact but denies that the second part is a guaranteed result.
