Partial Negation: ～わけではない

The expression ～わけではない is used to partially negate a statement or to clarify that while something might seem a certain way, it isn't entirely true. It translates to "it doesn't mean that...", "it's not that...", or "it's not necessarily the case that..."

1. Sentence Structure

Attach わけではない to the Plain Form of verbs and adjectives. For nouns, use という or な.

  • 日本料理にほんりょうりが嫌きらいなわけではありませんが、納豆なっとうは食たべられません。
    (It's not that I hate Japanese food, but I can't eat Natto.)
  • 暇ひまなわけではないけれど、ちょっとお茶ちゃでも飲のみましょう。
    (It's not that I'm free, but let's have some tea anyway.)
  • 高たかければいいというわけではない。
    (It's not necessarily true that if it's expensive, it's good.)

2. Nuance: Clarification

Use this when you want to avoid a misunderstanding. It softens a statement by showing that the obvious conclusion is not the correct one.
