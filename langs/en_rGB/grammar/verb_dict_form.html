Dictionary Form (辞書形じしょけい)

The Dictionary Form is the most basic form of a Japanese verb. It is the form you find when looking up words in a dictionary. In daily life, it is used for casual speech among friends and family.

1. The Three Verb Groups

Every Japanese verb belongs to one of three groups. Knowing the group is vital for any further conjugation.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

These verbs always end in an "u" sound in their dictionary form.

  • 買かう (to buy)
  • 聞きく (to listen/ask)
  • 話はなす (to speak)
  • 待まつ (to wait)
  • 飲のむ (to drink)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

These verbs always end in る, usually preceded by an "i" or "e" sound.

  • 食たべる (to eat)
  • 見みる (to see/watch)
  • 寝ねる (to sleep)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

There are only two main irregular verbs:

  • する (to do)
  • 来くる (to come)

2. Usage in Sentences

In casual Japanese, the dictionary form expresses the Present Affirmative or the Future.

  • 明日あした、映画えいがを見みる。
    (Tomorrow, I will watch a movie.)
  • 毎日まいにち、水みずを飲のむ。
    (Every day, I drink water.)
