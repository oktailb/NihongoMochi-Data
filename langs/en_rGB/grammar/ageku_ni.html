In the end (unfortunate result): ～あげく (に)

The expression ～あげく (written as ～挙げ句) is used to describe a result that occurs after a long period of effort, trouble, or worry. In most cases, the final result is negative or unfortunate.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach あげく to the Past Form (Ta-form) of a verb or to a Noun (using の).

[Verb た形たけい / Noun の] + あげく

  • いろいろ悩なやんだあげく、会社かいしゃを辞やめることにした。
    (After worrying about many things, I ended up deciding to quit the company.)
  • 何時間なんじかんも議論ぎろんしたあげくに、結論けつろんが出なかった。
    (After arguing for hours, in the end, no conclusion was reached.)
  • 口論こうろんのあげく、ついに取とっ手て合あいの喧嘩けんかになった。
    (After a verbal argument, it finally turned into a physical fight.)

2. Key Nuance: Long Struggle

This expression implies that the process leading up to the result was long, repetitive, or difficult. It often carries a sense of "after all that trouble, this is what happened."

3. Distinction from ～すえに

  • ～あげく: Usually leads to a negative or disappointing result.
  • ～すえに: Can lead to either positive or negative results, focusing on the final outcome after a long process.
