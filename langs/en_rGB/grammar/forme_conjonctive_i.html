The Conjunctive Base (Masu-stem): ～い

The Conjunctive Base (also known as the Masu-stem or V-base) is one of the most important building blocks in Japanese grammar. It is used to attach many suffixes and to connect sentences in formal writing.

1. How to Find the Stem

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound of the dictionary form to the corresponding "i" sound.

  • 行いく → 行いき
  • 飲のむ → 飲のみ
  • 話はなす → 話はなし

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Simply remove the final る.

  • 食たべる → 食たべ
  • 見みる → 見み

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する → し
  • 来くる → 来き (pronounced ki)

2. Common Uses

Once you have this base, you can build many expressions by adding suffixes:

  • Politeness: Stem + ます (e.g., 行いきます)
  • Desire: Stem + たい (e.g., 行いきたい)
  • Simultaneous: Stem + ながら (e.g., 飲のみながら)
  • Excess: Stem + すぎる (e.g., 食たべすぎる)

3. Formal Sentence Connection

In formal or written Japanese, this base is used to connect two sentences, functioning similarly to the Te-form but sounding more literary.

  • 朝起あさおき、コーヒーを飲のむ。
    (I wake up [formal connection] and drink coffee.)
