Just finished: ～たばかり

The expression ～たばかり is used to say that an action has just been completed. While similar to ～たところ, ～たばかり focuses more on the speaker's feeling that very little time has passed.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach ばかり to the Past Form (Ta-form) of a verb.

[Verb た形たけい] + ばかり

  • さっき、昼ひるごはんを食たべたばかりです。
    (I just ate lunch a moment ago.)
  • 日本にほんに来くたばかりのころは、日本語にほんごがわかりませんでした。
    (When I had just come to Japan, I didn't understand Japanese.)
  • このテレビは、先週せんしゅう買かったばかりです。
    (I just bought this TV last week [feels like just now].)

2. Difference from ～たところ

  • ～たところ: Used for things that happened seconds or minutes ago. It's a factual point in time.
  • ～たばかり: Can be used even if weeks or months have passed, as long as the speaker feels it was recent (e.g., "I just started this job last month").

3. Conjugation

～たばかり behaves like a Noun or a Na-adjective.

  • 買かったばかりの服ふく (Clothes I just bought)
  • 買かったばかりだ (I just bought it)
