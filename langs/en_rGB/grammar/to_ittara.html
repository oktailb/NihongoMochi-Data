Speaking of Extremes: ～といったら

Sometimes a situation is so extreme that words almost fail you! ～といったら is used to emphasize the degree of a state or emotion. It means "when you talk about X [it's so Y!]" or "the X was just [unbelievable!]."

1. Sentence Structure

Noun + といったら (ない)

2. Emphatic Examples

  • その美うつくしさといったら、言葉ことばでは表あらわせない。
    (Speaking of its beauty, words cannot express it / It was unimaginably beautiful.)
  • 試験しけんに落おちたときの悔くやしさといったらなかった。
    (The frustration when I failed the exam was beyond words / I couldn't have been more frustrated.)
  • 富士山ふじさんの頂上ちょうじょうから見みた景色のすばらしさといったら！
    (Oh, the magnificence of the view from the top of Mt. Fuji! [It was incredible!])

3. Sensei's Note

When you use ～といったら, you are setting the stage for a very strong description. If you add ない (or ari wa shinai) at the end, it means "there is nothing that matches X," making it the ultimate superlative. It's the "wow" factor of grammar!
