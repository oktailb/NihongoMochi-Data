The Risk of Danger: ～恐れがある (おそれがある)

Life isn't always smooth sailing. When there is a danger or a possibility of something bad happening, we use ～恐れがある. It means "there is a risk that" or "it is feared that." It's the serious, formal way to discuss negative possibilities.

1. Sentence Structure

Verb (Dictionary Form) / Noun + no + 恐れがある

2. Cautionary Examples

  • この台風たいふうは、日本にほんに上陸じょうりくする恐れがある。
    (There is a risk that this typhoon will make landfall in Japan.)
  • このままでは、会社かいしゃが倒産とうさんする恐れがある。
    (At this rate, there is a risk that the company will go bankrupt.)
  • 噴火ふんかの恐れがあるため、避難ひなんしてください。
    (Because there is a fear of an eruption, please evacuate.)

3. Sensei's Note

While ~kamoshirenai is for any possibility, ～恐れがある is only for bad things. You would never say "There is an osore that I'll win the lottery." It's often used in news, weather reports, and formal warnings to emphasize a potential hazard.
