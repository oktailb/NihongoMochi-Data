Receiving: もらう and いただく

In Japanese, verbs of "receiving" are used when you receive something from someone. These verbs place the receiver (usually you) as the main subject of the sentence, emphasizing your gratitude.

1. The Two Levels of Receiving

Verb Politeness Usage
いただく Humble (謙譲語けんじょうご) Receiving from a superior (boss, teacher, stranger).
もらう Standard/Casual Receiving from equals, friends, or family.

2. Basic Sentence Structure

The person you receive from is marked with に or から.

[Receiver] は [Giver] に/から [Object] を もらう/いただく。

  • 私わたしは友達ともだちにプレゼントをもらいました。
    (I received a present from my friend.)
  • 私わたしは先生せんせいに本ほんをいただきました。
    (I [humbly] received a book from my teacher.)

3. Receiving a Favor: ～てもらう / ～ていただく

This is extremely common. It is used when someone does something for you, and you are the beneficiary.

  • 友達ともだちに漢字かんじを教おしえてもらいました。
    (I had my friend teach me Kanji / My friend kindly taught me Kanji.)
  • 先生せんせいに直なおしていただきました。
    (I had the teacher correct [it] for me.)

4. Comparison: もらう vs くれる

Both describe the same event, but the subject (the person with the は particle) changes:

  • もらう: Focuses on the receiver. ("I got...")
    私わたしは田中たなかさんに本ほんをもらった。
  • くれる: Focuses on the giver. ("He gave me...")
    田中たなかさんは私わたしに本ほんをくれた。
