Reason / Excuse: ～もの / ～もん

In casual conversation, ～もの (or its more colloquial form ～もん) is placed at the end of a sentence to give a reason or an excuse. It often has a nuance of "but..." or "because..." and can sound slightly defensive or even cute/childish.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach it to the end of a sentence in its Plain Form.

  • 「どうして食たべないの？」
    「だって、おいしくないもん。」
    ("Why aren't you eating?" "Because it doesn't taste good [so there].")
  • 「まだ起おきているの？」
    「まだ宿題しゅくだいが終おわっていないんだもの。」
    ("Are you still awake?" "Well, my homework isn't finished yet.")

2. Nuance

It is often preceded by だって (But...) to emphasize the excuse. While common among children and women, it is used by anyone in very casual, close relationships to show a bit of stubbornness or to explain one's feelings.
