Tentative Intentions: ～(よう)と思う

In Japanese, stating your plans too directly can sometimes sound harsh. By using the Volitional Form plus と思う (to think), you express your intention in a softer, more reflective way: "I'm thinking of doing..."

1. Sentence Structure

[Verb Volitional Form] + と思う / と思っています

2. Thoughtful Examples

  • 来年らいねん、日本にほんへ行いこうと思おもいます。
    (I think I will go to Japan next year / I'm planning to go.)
  • 会社かいしゃを辞やめようと思おもっています。
    (I am considering quitting my job.)
  • もっと漢字かんじを勉強べんきょうしようと思おもった。
    (I decided/thought that I should study more Kanji.)

3. Sensei's Note

Using ～と思っています (continuous form) suggests that you have been thinking about this for a while and the decision is becoming firm. Use the simple ～と思う for an intention that just popped into your head!
