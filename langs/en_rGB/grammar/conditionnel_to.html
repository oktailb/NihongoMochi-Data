The "Natural" Conditional: ～と

The conditional ～と is used to express an inevitable or natural consequence. It means "whenever A happens, B always follows."

1. Key Functions

  • Natural Laws: Things that happen in nature.
  • Machine Operations: What happens when you press a button or turn a dial.
  • Directions: "If you turn right, you will see the station."
  • Habits: "Whenever I wake up, I drink coffee."

2. Sentence Structure

Attach と to the Dictionary Form of a verb or the plain form of an adjective.

  • 冬ふゆになると、雪ゆきが降ふります。
    (When winter comes, it snows.)
  • このボタンをおすと、お釣つりが出ます。
    (If you press this button, the change comes out.)
  • まっすぐ行いくと、銀行ぎんこうがあります。
    (If you go straight, there is a bank.)

3. Important Restriction

Because ～と expresses a natural consequence, the second part of the sentence cannot be a command, a request, or an expression of will (like "let's" or "I want to"). Use ～たら for those situations instead.
