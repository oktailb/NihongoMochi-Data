The "Just" Factor: ～ばかり (Just happened / Only)

In Japanese, ばかり is like a time-manipulation skill. It allows you to express that something happened "just now," even if your sense of "now" is a bit flexible. It can also act as a filter, meaning "nothing but" or "only."

"I just finished this boss fight!" (Even if it was 10 minutes ago, the adrenaline is still there!)

1. Mode: Just Finished (Verb た + ばかり)

Use this when an action is fresh in your mind. The cool thing is, you can use it for things that happened a while ago, as long as it feels recent to you.

  • 日本にほんに来くたばかりです。 (I just arrived in Japan — could be yesterday or last week!)
  • さっき起おきたばかりです。 (I just woke up.)

2. Mode: Nothing But (Noun + ばかり)

When you're stuck in a loop or surrounded by the same things, use ばかり to say "only" or "nothing but."

  • 漫画まんがばかり読よんでいる。 (Reading nothing but manga.)
  • 肉にくばかり食たべる。 (Eating only meat.)

3. The "Recent" Duel: ばかり vs ところ

Choosing your weapon correctly is key:

  • ～たところ: Precise timing. It happened seconds ago. Fact-based.
  • ～たばかり: Subjective timing. It's about how you feel. Emotion-based.

4. Grammar Skill Tree

ばかり conjugates like a noun. If it's modifying another noun, use の.

  • 買かったばかりの靴くつ (Shoes I just bought.)
Sensei's Note: Using ばかり for "only" can sometimes sound a bit critical, like a mom scolding you for playing nothing but video games. Use it wisely!

始はじめたばかりだけど、頑張がんばろう ! (We just started, but let's do our best!)
