Downplaying and Surprise: ～など / ～なんか / ～なんて

Japanese speakers use these particles to say "and things like that," but they also have a hidden emotional side! They can be used to downplay something (making it seem unimportant) or to express total disbelief or surprise.

1. Sentence Structure

  • Noun / Verb (Plain) + など (Formal)
  • Noun / Verb (Plain) + なんか (Casual/Dismissive)
  • Noun / Verb (Plain) + なんて (Expressive/Surprise)

2. Nuanced Examples

  • 私わたしなんかにできるでしょうか。
    (Could someone [humble/unimportant] like me really do it?)
  • お金かねなんていりません！
    (I don't need things like money! - Downplaying importance)
  • 彼かれが犯人はんにんだなんて信しんじられない！
    (I can't believe that [someone like] him is the criminal! - Surprise/Disbelief)

3. Sensei's Note

These particles are very "human." They show the speaker's attitude. なんか is often used for humility (pointing at yourself) or dismissal (pointing at something you don't like). なんて is the go-to for "I'm shocked!" moments. Use them to add flavor to your sentences!
