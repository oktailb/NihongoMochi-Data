Double Negative Nuance: ～ないことはない

In Japanese, two negatives don't always make a simple positive; they make a "weak positive" or a "soft yes." `～ないことはない` (written as `～ないことはない`) means "It's not that I don't..." or "It's not impossible." It's used when you want to agree but with some hesitation or conditions.

1. Sentence Structure

`[Plain Negative Form] + ことはない / こともない`

2. Hesitant Examples

  • 漢字かんじを書かけ`ないことはない`が、難むずかしいものは苦手にがてだ。
    (It's not that I can't write Kanji, but I'm not good at the difficult ones.)
  • お酒さけを飲のま`ないことはない`ですが、あまり好すきではありません。
    (It's not that I don't drink, but I don't really like it much.)
  • やってみれば、でき`ないことはない`はずだ。
    (If you try, it shouldn't be impossible to do.)

3. Sensei's Note

Japanese people often avoid saying "Yes" or "No" too strongly. This pattern allows you to say "Yes" in a very nuanced way. It implies: "Technically yes, but there's more to the story." Use it to sound more natural and polite in your replies!
