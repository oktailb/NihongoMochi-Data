Probability: ～でしょう and ～だろう

These expressions are used to express a guess or probability about something. They mean "probably," "I think," or "right?"

1. The Difference

  • ～でしょう: Polite form. Used in formal situations, weather forecasts, or when being polite to others.
  • ～だろう: Casual form. Used with friends, family, or by men in a slightly rougher way.

2. Basic Usage (Guessing)

Attach directly to the Plain Form of verbs and adjectives. For nouns and na-adjectives, do not add da.

  • 明日あしたは晴はれるでしょう。
    (Tomorrow will probably be sunny.)
  • この料理りょうりはおいしいだろう。
    (This food is probably delicious.)
  • 田中たなかさんは暇ひまだろう。
    (Mr. Tanaka is probably free.)

3. Seeking Confirmation (Right?)

When said with a rising intonation, it functions like a tag question ("...right?").

  • 明日あした、行いくでしょう？
    (You're going tomorrow, right?)

4. Summary of Connection

Type Connection
Verbs / い-Adj Plain Form + でしょう/だろう
な-Adj / Nouns Word (no da) + でしょう/だろう
