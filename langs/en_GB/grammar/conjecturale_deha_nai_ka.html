The Rhetorical Question: ～ではないか

Sometimes in Japanese, you don't ask a question to get an answer, but to suggest an idea, express surprise, or make a polite recommendation. This is the ～ではないか (or its more casual version ～じゃないか) pattern.

1. Sentence Structure

  • Verb/Adjective (Plain form) + ではないか / じゃないか
  • Noun + ではないか / じゃないか

2. Vivid Examples

  • これからみんなで頑張がんばろうではないか！
    (Shall we not all work hard together from now on? - Strong invitation/suggestion)
  • あそこにいるのは田中たなかさんじゃないか？
    (Isn't that Tanaka-san over there? - Expressing a realization)
  • この料理りょうり、おいしいじゃないか！
    (Hey, this food is actually delicious, isn't it! - Expressing pleasant surprise)

3. Sensei's Note

When used with the Volitional Form (like ganbarou), it becomes a very powerful, slightly formal "Let us...!" cry. In daily life, you'll hear ～じゃん in slangy Japanese, which is a very shortened version of this same feeling of "Look! Isn't it X?"
