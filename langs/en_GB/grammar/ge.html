The Look of Emotion: ～げ

How do we describe the "vibe" or the "look" someone has? The suffix `～げ` is attached to adjectives to describe an appearance that seems to reveal an inner feeling. It's like saying "seemingly" or "with an air of."

1. Sentence Structure

  • `い-Adj (remove ～い)` + げ
  • `な-Adj (remove ～な)` + げ

2. Expressive Examples

  • 彼かれは悲かなし`げ`に窓まどの外そとを見みていた。
    (He was looking out the window with a sad air / seemingly sad.)
  • 自信じしんあり`げ`な態度たいどで話はなす。
    (To speak with a confident air / appearing confident.)
  • 怪あやし`げ`な男おとこがこちらを見みている。
    (A suspicious-looking man is looking this way.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is more literary and sophisticated than the common `～そう`. While `～そう` is a general "looks like," `～げ` specifically focuses on `emotions` or `states of mind`. You can even use it with verbs sometimes, like `言いたげ` (looking like they want to say something).
