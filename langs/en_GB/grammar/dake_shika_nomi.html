Only: だけ, しか, and のみ

Japanese has several ways to say "only" or "just," each with its own nuance and grammatical rules.

1. だけ (Neutral "Only")

Used to indicate that something is enough or simply limited to a certain amount. It can be used in affirmative sentences.

  • りんごを一ひとつ`だけ`食たべました。
    (I ate only one apple.)

2. しか (Negative "Only")

Always used with a `negative verb`. It emphasizes that the amount is `not enough` or surprisingly small. It means "nothing but."

  • りんごを一ひとつ`しか`食たべませんでした。
    (I ate `nothing but` one apple / I `only` ate one.)

3. のみ (Formal "Only")

This is the formal and written equivalent of `だけ`. You will see it on signs, in manuals, or in formal speeches.

  • この駐車場ちゅうしゃじょうは契約者けいやくしゃ`のみ`利用りようできます。
    (Only contract holders can use this parking lot.)

4. Comparison Summary

Word Verb Type Nuance
`だけ` Affirmative Neutral limit.
`しか` `Negative` Insufficiency / Surprise.
`のみ` Affirmative Formal / Written.
