Strong Negative Will: ～まい

In formal or literary Japanese, we have a special word for "will not" or "probably not." `～まい` is the negative counterpart to the volitional form (`~おう/~よう`). It expresses a strong determination not to do something, or a conjecture that something probably won't happen.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Group 1 Verbs:` Dictionary form + まい (e.g., 書くまい)
  • `Group 2 Verbs:` Stem + まい OR Dictionary form + まい (e.g., 食べまい / 食べるまい)
  • `Irregular:` くる → こまい / くるまい, する → すまい / するまい / しまい

2. Resolute Examples

  • あんな店みせ、二度と行いく`まい`。
    (I shall never go to that kind of shop again! - `Strong will`)
  • この計画けいかくはだれにも知しられ`まい`。
    (This plan will probably not be known by anyone. - `Strong conjecture`)
  • もう嘘うそはつく`まい`と心こころに決きめた。
    (I decided in my heart that I would not tell lies anymore.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a "stiff" and formal expression. You won't hear it much in casual chat, but you will see it in books and hear it in dramatic speeches. It carries a heavy weight of finality. When you say `行くまい`, you aren't just saying "I'm not going," you're saying "I have zero intention of going!"
