Ongoing Actions & States: ~ている

The `~て　いる` structure is one of the most frequently used patterns in Japanese. Depending on the verb, it describes either an action currently in progress or a lasting state resulting from a past action.

1. How to Form It

Take the `て形` of any verb and add the verb `いる` (iru - to exist/be).

  • 読よむ → 読よんで → `読よんでいる` (Reading)
  • 食たべる → 食たべて → `食たべている` (Eating)

2. Two Main Meanings

A. Action in Progress (Continuous)

With "action verbs" like eating, reading, or running, it means you are doing it `right now`.

  • 今いま、本ほんを`読よんでいます`。
    (I am reading a book now.)

B. Resultant State

With "change-of-state verbs" like getting married, waking up, or sitting down, it describes a `state that continues` after the action happened.

  • 結婚けっこん`しています`。
    (I am married.)
  • 知し`っています`。
    (I know.)
  • 窓まどが閉し`まっています`。
    (The window is closed.)

3. Casual vs. Polite

You can conjugate `いる` to match the politeness level you need:

  • `Casual:` 食たべている
  • `Polite:` 食たべて`います`
  • `Casual Past:` 食たべていた
  • `Polite Past:` 食たべて`いました`

4. Casual "i" Dropping

In casual spoken Japanese, the `い (i)` is often dropped.

  • 何なにして`る`の？
    (What are you doing?)
