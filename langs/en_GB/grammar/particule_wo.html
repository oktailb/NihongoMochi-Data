The Particle を (Object Marker)

The particle を (written as "wo" but pronounced "o") marks the direct object of a verb. It tells you what is being acted upon.

1. Function

In English, we know the object by its position after the verb (e.g., "Eat an apple"). In Japanese, the object can be anywhere before the verb, so we attach を to it to identify it.

2. Key Examples

  • 水みずを飲のみます。
    (I drink water.)
  • 本ほんを読よみます。
    (I read a book.)
  • 音楽おんがくを聞ききます。
    (I listen to music.)

3. Important Tip: Pronunciation

Although it is typed as wo on a keyboard, the "w" is silent in modern Japanese. It sounds exactly like the vowel o (お). This specific hiragana を is used only as a particle.

4. Common Pitfall

Be careful! Some verbs that take "to" or "at" in English (like "meet someone" or "ride a bus") don't use を in Japanese. Always check the specific particle used by each verb.
