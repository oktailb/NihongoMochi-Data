Absolute Impossibility: ～っこない

The suffix `～っこない` is a casual and emphatic way to say that something is absolutely impossible or will definitely not happen. It reflects the speaker's strong conviction.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `っこない` to the `Verb Stem` (the part before `~ます`).

`[Verb Stem] + っこない`

  • こんなにたくさんの漢字かんじ、一日いちにちで覚おぼえ`っこない`。
    (It's `absolutely impossible` to memorize this many Kanji in one day.)
  • かれが時間じかんどおりに来く`っこない`よ。
    (There's `no way` he'll come on time.)
  • 宝たからくじなんて当あたり`っこない`。
    (There's `no chance` of winning the lottery.)

2. Nuance

This is a very subjective and informal expression. It sounds quite strong and is mostly used in spoken language with friends or family to dismiss a possibility.

3. Distinction from ～わけがない

  • `～わけがない:` Logical impossibility (Based on facts).
  • `～っこない:` Emotional/Subjective impossibility (Strong personal opinion).
