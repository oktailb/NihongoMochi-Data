Hypothetical Scenarios: ～としたら / ～とすれば

What if...? `～としたら` (and its variation `～とすれば`) is used to set up a pure hypothesis. It means "Assuming that X is true," or "If we were to suppose X." it's used for scenarios that aren't necessarily real yet.

1. Sentence Structure

`[Verb/Adj/Noun Plain Form] + としたら / とすれば`

2. Imaginative Examples

  • 宝たからくじが当たった`としたら`、何を買いますか？
    (If [assuming] you won the lottery, what would you buy?)
  • 明日、世界が終わる`とすれば`、何をしますか？
    (If the world were to end tomorrow, what would you do?)
  • その話が本当だ`としたら`、大変なことだ。
    (If that story is true, it's a huge deal.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is the "Director's" conditional. You are setting the stage for a "what if" game. While `~たら` is for more likely events, `～としたら` is often used for things that are unlikely, imaginary, or purely for the sake of an argument. It's the language of imagination!
