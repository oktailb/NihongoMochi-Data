On Top of That: ～上に (うえに)

Sometimes one good thing (or one bad thing) is just the beginning! `～上に` means "on top of," "moreover," or "besides." It's used to stack qualities or situations that all point in the same direction.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Verb/Adj (Plain Form)` + 上に
  • `Noun` + の上に
  • `な-Adj` + な / である + 上に

2. Stacking Examples

  • この店みせは料理りょうりがおいしい`上に`、値段ねだんも安やすい。
    (Not only is the food delicious in this shop, but on top of that, the prices are cheap.)
  • 彼かれは歌うたが上手じょうずな`上に`、楽器がっきもできる。
    (He is a good singer and, moreover, he can play instruments.)
  • 今日は道みちが込こんでいる`上に`、雨あめも降ふってきた。
    (On top of the roads being crowded today, it also started to rain.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a great way to build an argument or a description. It's like adding extra weight to one side of a scale. Be careful: you usually use it to stack two positive things OR two negative things. Don't mix them up, or it will sound strange!
