Existence: ある and いる

In Japanese, there are two verbs that mean "to be" or "to exist." The one you use depends on whether the subject is `living` or `non-living`.

1. ある (Non-living things)

Use `ある` for inanimate objects, plants, or abstract concepts (ideas, events).

  • 本ほんが`あります`。
    (There is a book.)
  • 明日あした、テストが`あります`。
    (Tomorrow, there is a test.)
  • 花はなが`あります`。
    (There is a flower.)

2. いる (Living things)

Use `いる` for people and animals (things that move by their own will).

  • 猫ねこが`います`。
    (There is a cat.)
  • 学生がくせいが`います`。
    (There is a student.)
  • 友達ともだちが`います`。
    (I have a friend / There is a friend.)

3. Sentence Structure

To say `where` something is, use the particle `に` for the location.

`[Location] に [Subject] が あります/います。`

  • 机つくえの上うえに本ほんが`あります`。
    (There is a book on the desk.)
  • 庭にわに犬いぬが`います`。
    (There is a dog in the garden.)

4. Quick Summary

Verb Type of Subject Examples
`ある` Inanimate Keys, Car, Party, Tree
`いる` Animate Person, Dog, Fish, Teacher
