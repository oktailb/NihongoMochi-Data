The Explanatory Form: ～んです / ～のです

The expression ～んです (polite) or ～のだ (casual) is used to provide an explanation, ask for a reason, or add emphasis to what you are saying. It is one of the most common structures in conversational Japanese.

1. When to use it?

  • Giving a Reason: When someone asks "Why?", you answer with ～んです.
  • Asking for Clarification: To ask "What's happening?" or "Why?".
  • Adding Emphasis: To show that you are very certain or passionate about something.

2. Sentence Structure

Attach it to the Plain Form. For nouns and na-adjectives in the present affirmative, add な.

  • Verbs: 行いくんです (I'm going [explanation]).
  • い-Adj: 暑あついんです (It's because it's hot).
  • な-Adj: 暇ひまなんです (It's because I'm free).
  • Nouns: 病気びょうきなんです (It's because I'm sick).

3. Key Examples

  • 「どうしたんですか。」
    「頭あたまが痛いたいんです。」
    ("What's the matter?" "I have a headache [that's why I look like this].")
  • 「どこへ行いくんですか。」
    「スーパーへ行いくんです。」
    ("Where are you going?" "I'm going to the supermarket.")

4. Note on Nuance

Using ～んです makes your sentence sound like an explanation for a visible situation. Without it, the sentence is just a flat statement of fact.
