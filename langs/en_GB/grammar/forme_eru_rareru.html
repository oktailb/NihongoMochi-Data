The Potential Form (可能形かのうけい) - "Can do"

The `Potential Form` is used to express that someone has the ability to do something, or that a situation allows for an action to happen. It is more casual and direct than `こと　が　できる`.

1. How to Conjugate

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final `"u"` sound to the corresponding `"e"` sound and add `る`.

  • 行いく → 行い`ける` (Can go)
  • 話はなす → 話はな`せる` (Can speak)
  • 飲のむ → 飲の`める` (Can drink)
  • 待まつ → 待ま`てる` (Can wait)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Remove the final `る` and add `られる`.

  • 食たべる → `食たべられる` (Can eat)
  • 見みる → `見みられる` (Can see)

`💡 Pro Tip:` In casual speech, Japanese people often drop the "ra" and say `食たべれる` (Ra-nuki kotoba).

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する → `できる` (Can do)
  • 来くる → `来こられる` (Can come)

2. The を to が Switch

When you use the potential form, the object of the action is usually marked with `が` instead of を.

  • 日本語にほんごを話はなします。(I speak Japanese.)
  • 日本語にほんご`が`話はなせます。(I `can` speak Japanese.)

3. Usage Note

Potential form verbs are always `Group 2 (Ichidan)` verbs. This means they conjugate like `たべる` for their past, negative, and polite forms.

  • 漢字かんじが書かけます (Polite)
  • 漢字かんじが書かけない (Negative)
