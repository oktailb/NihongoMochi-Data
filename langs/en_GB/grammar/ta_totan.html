Just as / The moment: ～たとたん（に）

The expression `～たとたん` is used to describe an action or change that happens immediately after another action. It often implies that the second event was sudden or unexpected.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `とたん` or `とたんに` to the `Past Form (Ta-form)` of a verb.

`[Verb た形たけい] + とたん（に）`

  • 窓まどを開あけた`とたん`、冷つめたい風かぜが入はいってきました。
    (The moment I opened the window, a cold wind came in.)
  • お酒さけを飲のんだ`とたん`、顔かおが赤あかくなりました。
    (Just as soon as I drank the alcohol, my face turned red.)
  • 立たち上あがった`とたん`、目めがくらみました。
    (The moment I stood up, I felt dizzy.)

2. Key Nuance: Unexpected Change

This structure is used for things the speaker cannot control. The second part of the sentence `cannot` express the speaker's will, request, or intention (like "let's do" or "please do").

3. Comparison: ～たとたん vs ～しだい

  • `～しだい:` Focuses on an intentional plan ("as soon as X happens, I will do Y").
  • `～たとたん:` Focuses on an immediate, often surprising reaction or change.
