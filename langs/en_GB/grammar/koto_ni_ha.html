The Essential Condition: ～ことには

Sometimes, B is impossible until A happens. `～ことには` is used to express a strict condition, often followed by a negative result. It means "Unless... is done, then... cannot be."

1. Sentence Structure

Usually used in the form: `[Verb Nai Form] + ことには` + [Negative Outcome]

2. Logical Examples

  • やってみない`ことには`、できるかどうかわからない。
    (Unless you try it, you won't know if you can do it or not.)
  • 実物じつぶつを見みない`ことには`、買かうとは決きめられない。
    (Unless I see the actual item, I can't decide to buy it.)
  • もっと安やすくならない`ことには`、売うれないだろう。
    (Unless it gets cheaper, it probably won't sell.)

3. Sensei's Note

Think of this as "The barrier." The first part is the barrier you must cross before the second part can change from "impossible" to "possible." It's a very clear way to state a prerequisite!
