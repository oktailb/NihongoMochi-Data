Giving Advice: ～ほうがいい

To give advice or suggestions, Japanese uses the structure ～ほうがいい. Depending on whether you suggest doing or avoiding something, the verb form changes.

1. Suggesting an Action (Affirmative)

Use the Past Tense (Ta-form). Even though you are giving advice for the future, the Ta-form is used to express that the choice is "already decided" as the better one.

[Verb た形たけい] + ほうがいいです

  • 薬くすりを飲のんだほうがいいです。
    (You should take medicine. / It's better to take medicine.)
  • もっと早はやく寝ねたほうがいいですよ。
    (It would be better to go to bed earlier, you know.)

2. Suggesting Avoidance (Negative)

Use the Negative Form (Nai-form).

[Verb ない形ないけい] + ほうがいいです

  • お酒さけを飲のまないほうがいいです。
    (You shouldn't drink alcohol. / It's better not to drink.)
  • そんなに心配しんぱいしないほうがいいですよ。
    (You'd better not worry so much.)

3. Nuance Note

This expression can sound quite strong or direct. When speaking to a superior, it is often better to use a softer expression like ～たらどうですか (How about...).
