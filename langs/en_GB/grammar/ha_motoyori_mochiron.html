Needless to Say: ～はもとより / ～はもちろん

When you want to emphasize that something is so obvious it doesn't even need mentioning, you use `～はもとより` or `～はもちろん`. It's a way to say "A is a given, and B is also true!"

1. Sentence Structure

`Noun` + はもとより / はもちろん (+ `も` in the second part)

2. Emphatic Examples

  • 彼かれは、英語えいご`はもとより`フランス語ご`も`話はなせる。
    (He can speak French, not to mention English / He speaks English as a matter of course, and French too.)
  • この映画えいがは、日本にほん`はもちろん`、世界せかい中じゅうで人気にんきがある。
    (This movie is popular all over the world, not just in Japan.)
  • 病気びょうきのとき、薬くすり`はもとより`栄養えいようのある食事しょくじ`も`大切たいせつだ。
    (When sick, nutritious meals are important, not to mention medicine.)

3. Sensei's Note

`～はもとより` is slightly more formal and written than `～はもちろん`. Both serve the same purpose: taking a basic fact for granted to highlight a second, perhaps more impressive, point.
