Although: ～ものの

The expression `～ものの` is used to connect two clauses when the second clause expresses something contrary to what the first clause suggests. It translates to "although," "but," or "even though." It is a formal expression, often used in writing or polite speech.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `ものの` to the `Plain Form` of verbs and adjectives. For nouns, use `であるものの` or `は～ものの`.

`[Plain Form] + ものの`

  • 車くるまを買かった`ものの`、あまり使つかっていません。
    (Although I bought a car, I don't use it very much.)
  • 日本語にほんごの勉強べんきょうを始はじめた`ものの`、なかなか上手じょうずになりません。
    (Although I started studying Japanese, I'm not becoming proficient very easily.)
  • 春はるとはいう`ものの`、まだ寒さむい日ひが続つづいています。
    (Even though it is said to be spring, cold days are still continuing.)

2. Key Nuance: Admitting a Fact

It carries a nuance of "I admit that A is true, but surprisingly/unfortunately B is the actual situation." It is often used to express a slight dissatisfaction or a gap between expectation and reality.
