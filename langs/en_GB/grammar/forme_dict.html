Dictionary Form (辞書形じしょけい)

The `Dictionary Form` is the most basic form of a Japanese verb. It is the form you will find in a dictionary and is used for casual speech with friends and family.

1. The Three Verb Groups

Every Japanese verb belongs to one of three groups. Knowing which group a verb belongs to is essential for conjugation.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

These verbs always end in an `"u"` sound.

  • 買かう (to buy)
  • 待まつ (to wait)
  • 行いく (to go)
  • 飲のむ (to drink)
  • 話はす (to speak)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

These verbs end in `る`, usually preceded by an "i" or "e" sound.

  • 食たべる (to eat)
  • 見みる (to see)
  • 寝ねる (to sleep)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

There are only two main irregular verbs:

  • する (to do)
  • 来くる (to come)

2. Casual Usage

In casual Japanese, the dictionary form expresses the `Present` or `Future` tense.

  • 明日あした、日本にほんに行いく。
    (Tomorrow, I will go to Japan.)
  • 毎日まいにち、水みずを飲のむ。
    (Every day, I drink water.)

3. Grammar Building Blocks

The dictionary form is required for many structures, such as:

  • `～ことができる` (can do...)
  • `～つもりです` (plan to...)
  • `～前まえに` (before doing...)
