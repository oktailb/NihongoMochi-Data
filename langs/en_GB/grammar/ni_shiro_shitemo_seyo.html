Regardless of Choice: ～にしろ / ～にしても / ～にせよ

Sometimes the result remains the same no matter which path you take. These expressions mean "even if," "whether... or...," or "regardless of." They are used to show that a conclusion applies to multiple possibilities.

1. Sentence Structure

  • Verb/Adj (Plain Form) + にしろ / にしても / にせよ
  • Noun + にしろ / にしても / にせよ

2. Universal Examples

  • 行いくにしても行いかないにしても、早はやめに連絡れんらくしてください。
    (Whether you go or not, please contact me early.)
  • 冗談じょうだんにしろ、そんなことを言いうのはよくない。
    (Even if it was a joke, it's not good to say such a thing.)
  • 忙いそがしいにせよ、食事しょくじはきちんと摂とるべきだ。
    (Even if you're busy, you should eat properly.)

3. Sensei's Note

These three are similar, but ～にせよ is the most formal, while ～にしても is the most common in daily life. When you use them in pairs (A ni shiro B ni shiro), you are saying that the outcome is true for both A and B. It's a great way to show that a rule or advice is unavoidable!
