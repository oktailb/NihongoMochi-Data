Limits and Extents: ～だけ (Quantity/Degree)

We all know `だけ` means "only," but it has a secret power! It can also mean "as much as" or "to the extent of." It's used to push an action to its natural limit.

1. Sentence Structure

Often paired with potential verbs (can do) or words like `たい` (want to).

  • `Verb (Plain Form)` + だけ
  • `Adjective` + だけ

2. Limit-Pushing Examples

  • 食たべたい`だけ`、食たべていいよ。
    (Eat as much as you want / Eat to your heart's content.)
  • やる`だけ`のことはやった。結果けっかを待まとう。
    (I've done all that I could do. Let's wait for the results.)
  • 試験しけんの前まえに、できる`だけ`勉強べ勉強します。
    (I will study as much as possible before the exam.)

3. Sensei's Note

Think of this `だけ` as a "boundary." You are filling up the space all the way to that boundary. `好きなだけ` (as much as you like) is a very common and welcoming phrase you'll hear at "all-you-can-eat" restaurants (tabehoudai)!
