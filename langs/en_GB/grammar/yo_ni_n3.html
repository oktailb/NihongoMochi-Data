In order to: ～ように

In Japanese, there are two main ways to say "in order to." While `～ために` is used for direct goals, `～ように` is used when the goal is something you cannot entirely control, like an ability or a state.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `ように` to the `Dictionary Form` (for potential or non-volitional verbs) or the `Nai-form`.

`[Goal / State] ように、[Action]`

  • 日本語にほんごが話はなせる`ように`、毎日まいにち練習れんしゅうします。
    (I practice every day `so that` I can speak Japanese [potential goal].)
  • 忘わすれない`ように`、メモをします。
    (I will take notes `so that` I don't forget [negative goal].)
  • みんなに聞きこえる`ように`、大きな声こえで話はなしてください。
    (Please speak in a loud voice `so that` everyone can hear.)

2. Comparison: ように vs ために

  • `～ために:` Used for `intentional actions` (e.g., saving money to buy a car). The subject has direct control over the goal.
  • `～ように:` Used for `states or abilities` (e.g., studying so you `can` speak). It describes creating a certain condition.

3. Connection Rule

Usually, `ように` is used with:

  • Potential verbs (話せる, 食べられる)
  • Nai-form (行かない, 忘れない)
  • Non-volitional verbs (聞こえる, 見える, わかる)
