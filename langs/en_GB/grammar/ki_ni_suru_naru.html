Caring and Worrying: 気にする and 気になる

Both expressions involve the word `気` (spirit/mind) and describe being concerned or worried about something, but they differ in how much control the person has over the feeling.

1. Conscious Concern: 気にする

This is used when someone `intentionally` worries or pays attention to something. It is an active choice.

  • 失敗しっぱいを`気にしないで`ください。
    (Please don't worry about the mistake.)
  • かれは周まわりの目めをとても`気にしています`。
    (He cares a lot about what others think [the eyes of others].)

2. Spontaneous Concern: 気になる

This is used when a thought or feeling `comes to mind naturally` or bothers you without you trying. It is more about a state of being curious or anxious.

  • テストの結果けっかが`気になります`。
    (I'm worried/curious about the test results [they are on my mind].)
  • さっきからあの音おとが`気になって`、勉強べんきょうできません。
    (That sound has been bothering me for a while, so I can't study.)
