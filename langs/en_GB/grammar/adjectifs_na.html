Na-Adjectives (な形容詞なけいようし)

The second type of Japanese adjectives is the `Na-adjective`. Unlike I-adjectives, these behave more like nouns and require the particle `な` when describing a noun.

1. Identifying Na-Adjectives

They often describe states, feelings, or qualities. They do `not` end in a conjugatable "i".

  • 静しずか (Quiet)
  • 有名ゆうめい (Famous)
  • 元気げんき (Healthy / Energetic)
  • 暇ひま (Free / Not busy)
  • きれい (Beautiful / Clean) `(⚠️ Caution: Looks like an I-adjective!)`

2. Conjugation (Polite)

Na-adjectives do not change their endings. Instead, you conjugate the word `です` that follows them.

Tense Ending Example (静しずか)
Present Affirmative です 静しずかです (It is quiet)
Present Negative ではありません 静しずかではありません (It is not quiet)
Past Affirmative でした 静しずかでした (It was quiet)
Past Negative ではありませんでした 静しずかではありませんでした

3. The "Na" Rule

This is why they are called `Na`-adjectives: when you put them right before a noun, you `must` add `な`.

  • 静しずか`な`町まち
    (A quiet town)
  • 有名ゆうめい`な`人ひと
    (A famous person)

4. Summary of Difference

  • `I-Adjective:` 高たかい車くるま (No extra particle needed)
  • `Na-Adjective:` 静しずか`な`町まち (Needs `な`)
