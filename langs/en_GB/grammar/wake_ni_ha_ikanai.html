"Cannot afford to...": ～わけにはいかない

The expression `～わけにはいかない` is used to say that you cannot do something due to social norms, a sense of duty, or psychological pressure—even if you might physically be able to or want to do it.

1. Cannot do (Affirmative connection)

Attach to the `Dictionary Form` of a verb. It means "I cannot do X because it would be wrong/irresponsible."

  • 明日あしたは試験しけんがあるから、休やすむ`わけにはいかない`。
    (I have an exam tomorrow, so I `cannot afford to [I must not]` take a day off.)
  • 車くるまで来きたので、お酒さけを飲のむ`わけにはいきません`。
    (Since I came by car, I `cannot possibly` drink alcohol.)

2. Must do (Negative connection)

Attach to the `Nai-form` of a verb (～ないわけにはいかない). It means "I must do X because it's my duty."

  • 大切たいせつな会議かいぎなので、行いか`ないわけにはいかない`。
    (Since it's an important meeting, I `have no choice but to go`.)

3. Nuance: Social Responsibility

This structure is deeply rooted in social expectations. You use it when your conscience or your role in society prevents you from acting differently.
