Contrast and Regret: ～のに

The conjunction `～のに` is used to connect two clauses when the second part is unexpected or contrary to what one would assume from the first part. It often carries a nuance of surprise, dissatisfaction, or regret.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `のに` to the `Plain Form` of verbs and adjectives. For nouns and na-adjectives in the present affirmative, add `な`.

  • `Verbs:` 一生懸命いっしょうけんめい勉強べんきょうした`のに`、テストがダメでした。
    (Even though I studied very hard, the test was a failure.)
  • `い-Adj:` 安やすい`のに`、とてもおいしいです。
    (Despite being cheap, it is very delicious.)
  • `な-Adj:` 静しずか`なのに`、眠ねむれません。
    (Even though it is quiet, I cannot sleep.)
  • `Nouns:` 日曜日にちようび`なのに`、仕事しごとがあります。
    (Even though it's Sunday, I have work.)

2. Nuance vs ～ても

  • `～ても:` Focuses on a hypothetical condition ("Even if...").
  • `～のに:` Focuses on a real fact that has happened or is true, usually expressing that the speaker is bothered by the result ("Despite the fact that...").
