Casual Lists and Rumors: ～とか

When you want to list a few examples without being too formal, or when you want to report a rumor you're not 100% sure about, `～とか` is your go-to particle! It's like a more relaxed version of `や　/　など`.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun A` とか `Noun B` (とか) (A, B, and so on)
  • `Sentence` + とか (I heard that... / Something like...)

2. Versatile Examples

  • 机つくえの上うえに本ほん`とか`ペン`とか`があります。
    (There are books, pens, and such on the desk.)
  • 彼かれは来週らいしゅう旅行りょこうに行いく`とか`言いっていました。
    (He was saying something about going on a trip next week.)
  • 最近さいきんはこの店みせが人気にんきだ`とか`。
    (I heard that this shop is popular lately [but I'm not sure].)

3. Sensei's Note

`～とか` is the king of "vague examples." It allows you to give a sample of what you mean without committing to an exhaustive list. When used at the end of a sentence for rumors, it adds a layer of uncertainty—as if you're passing on a "whisper" you heard!
