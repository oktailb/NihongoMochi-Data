The Causative Form (使役形しえきけい)

The `Causative Form` is used to say that you "make" or "let" someone do something. It can express both a command (making someone do) or permission (letting someone do).

1. How to Conjugate

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final `"u"` sound to the `"a"` sound and add `せる`.

  • 行いく (to go) → 行い`かせる` (make/let go)
  • 飲のむ (to drink) → 飲の`ませる` (make/let drink)
  • 話はなす (to speak) → 話はな`させる` (make/let speak)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Remove the final `る` and add `させる`.

  • 食たべる → `食たべさせる` (make/let eat)
  • 見みる → `見みさせる` (make/let see)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する → `させる`
  • 来くる → `来こさせる` (pronounced `こ-させる`)

2. Sentence Structure

The person who is made/let to do the action is marked with `を` (for intransitive verbs) or `に` (for transitive verbs).

  • 母ははは子供こどもに野菜やさいを`食たべさせました`。
    (The mother made the child eat vegetables.)
  • 先生せんせいは私わたしを早はやく`帰かえらせてくれました`。
    (The teacher let me go home early.)

3. "Make" vs "Let"

The causative form itself doesn't distinguish between making and letting. The context or the addition of `～てくれる` (to do a favor) clarifies it.

  • `行いかせる:` Make someone go.
  • `行いかせてくれる:` Let someone go (as a favor).
