Rather Than: ～というより

Sometimes a word is "close" but not quite right. `～というより` is used when you want to refine a description by saying "X is more accurately described as Y rather than X." It's like saying "Instead of calling it X, it's more like Y."

1. Sentence Structure

`A` というより `B` (B rather than A)

2. Refined Examples

  • 彼かれは友達ともだち`というより`、家族かぞくのような存在そんざいだ。
    (He is more like family than just a friend.)
  • この料理りょうりは、おいしい`というより`辛からい。
    (This dish is spicy rather than delicious / spicy more than anything else.)
  • 歩あるく`というより`、走はしっているような速はやさだ。
    (He's going at a speed that's more like running than walking.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a wonderful way to be precise! It shows that you are thinking carefully about the best way to describe a situation. It's often used when Factor A is technically true, but Factor B captures the `essence` of the situation much better.
