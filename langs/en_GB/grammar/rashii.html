Conjecture: ～らしい

The expression `～らしい` is used to express a conjecture or guess based on information you have heard or observed. It translates to "it seems that..." or "I heard that..."

1. Based on Information

Use `～らしい` when you are making a guess based on something you read, heard, or some indirect evidence.

  • 天気予報てんきよほうによると、明日あしたは雨あめ`らしい`です。
    (According to the weather forecast, it seems it will rain tomorrow.)
  • 田中たなかさんは日本にほんに行いった`らしい`です。
    (I heard that Mr. Tanaka went to Japan.)

2. Typical Characteristics

When used with a noun, it can also mean "typical of" or "just like a...".

  • 今日きょうは春はる`らしい`天気てんきですね。
    (Today's weather is just like spring, isn't it?)

3. Connection

It attaches directly to the `Plain Form` of verbs and adjectives, and directly to `Nouns`.

Type Connection
Verbs / い-Adj Plain Form + らしい
な-Adj / Nouns Word (no `だ`) + らしい

4. rashii vs so

  • `～そう:` Based on your direct visual observation (I see dark clouds, it looks like rain).
  • `～らしい:` Based on indirect evidence or hearsay (The news said it might rain).
