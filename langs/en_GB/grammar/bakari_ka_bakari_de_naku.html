Not Only A, But Also B: ～ばかりか / ～ばかりでなく

Listen closely! Sometimes, saying just one thing isn't enough to describe the greatness (or the disaster) of a situation. When you want to say "Not only X, but also Y," we use `～ばかりか` or `～ばかりでなく`.

1. Sentence Structure

This is often paired with `も` (also) in the second part of the sentence to emphasize the addition.

  • `Noun` + ばかりか / ばかりでなく
  • `Verb (Plain Form)` + ばかりか / ばかりでなく
  • `い-Adj` + ばかりか / ばかりでなく
  • `な-Adj` + な + ばかりか / ばかりでなく

2. Memorable Examples

  • 彼かれは漢字かんじが書かける`ばかりか`、難むずかしい小説しょうせつも読よめます。
    (Not only can he write Kanji, but he can also read difficult novels!)
  • この店みせは味あじが悪わるい`ばかりでなく`、態度たいどもひどい。
    (Not only is the food bad in this shop, but the staff's attitude is also terrible!)
  • 彼女かのじょは歌うたが上手じょうずな`ばかりか`、ダンスもプロ級きゅうだ。
    (Not only is she good at singing, her dancing is also professional level!)

3. Sensei's Note

`～ばかりか` feels a bit more formal and emphasizes the "unexpected" nature of the second part more than `～ばかりでなく`. Use it when you want to sound impressed or truly shocked!
