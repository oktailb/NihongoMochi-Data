Expressing Ability: ～ことができる

To say that you "can" or "have the ability to" do something in Japanese, you use the structure: `[Verb Dictionary Form] + ことができる`.

1. Sentence Structure

Take the `Dictionary Form` of any verb, add the noun `こと` (which turns the verb into a "thing"), and then use `が出来できる` (can / is possible).

  • 日本語にほんごを話はなす`ことができる`。
    (I can speak Japanese.)
  • 車くるまを運転うんてんする`ことができます`。
    (I can drive a car.)

2. Key Examples

  • 漢字かんじを書かく`ことができます`。
    (I can write Kanji.)
  • ピアノを弾ひく`ことができません`。
    (I cannot play the piano.)

3. Usage in Questions

You can ask about someone's skills by adding the question particle `か`.

  • 泳およぐ`ことができますか`。
    (Can you swim?)

4. Note on Potential Form

As you progress to N4, you will learn a shorter way to express ability (the Potential Form). However, `～ことができる` is the standard, clear way to express ability at the N5 level and is very common in both speech and writing.
