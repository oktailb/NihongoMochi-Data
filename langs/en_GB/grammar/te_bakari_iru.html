Doing Nothing But: ～てばかりいる

We all have those days where we just can't seem to stop doing one particular (and usually unproductive) thing. ～てばかりいる is used to say someone is "doing nothing but X" or "always doing X." It often carries a nuance of criticism or complaint.

1. Sentence Structure

[Verb Te-form] + ばかりいる

2. Nagging Examples

  • 弟おとうとはゲームをしてばかりいる。
    (My little brother does nothing but play video games.)
  • 最近さいきん、雨あめが降ふってばかりいる。
    (Lately, it's been doing nothing but raining.)
  • 遊あそんでばかりいないで、たまには勉強べんきょうしなさい。
    (Don't just play all the time; study once in a while!)

3. Sensei's Note

This is different from ~te iru (continuous) or yoku ~suru (often). ～てばかりいる implies that the person is neglecting other things to do X. It feels like a spotlight is shining on one repetitive action while everything else is ignored. Use it when you're a bit frustrated with someone's habits!
