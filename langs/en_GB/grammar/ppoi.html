Casual Resemblance: ～っぽい

Sometimes something isn't exactly X, but it feels very "X-ish." `～っぽい` is a very common, casual suffix that means "-ish," "like," or "appearing to be." It can also describe a tendency to do something (like getting angry easily).

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun` + っぽい
  • `Verb Stem` + っぽい
  • `い-Adj (remove ～い)` + っぽい

2. Vivid Examples

  • この服ふく、ちょっと子供こども`っぽい`かな？
    (Does this outfit look a bit childish / kid-ish?)
  • 彼かれは怒おこり`っぽい`から、気きをつけて。
    (He gets angry easily [is "angry-ish"], so be careful.)
  • このスープは水みず`っぽい`ね。
    (This soup is watery, isn't it?)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a very "street-level" Japanese word! It's less formal than `~らしい` or `~みたい`. It's often used when an impression is a bit critical or informal. If someone is acting like a child, they are `子供っぽい`. If a boy looks like a girl, he might be called `女っぽい`. Use it when you want to describe a "vibe" quickly!
