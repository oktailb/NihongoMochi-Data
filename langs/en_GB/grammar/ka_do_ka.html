Indirect Questions: ～か and ～かどうか

In Japanese, you can embed a question inside a larger sentence to say things like "I don't know if..." or "I'll check whether...". The form you use depends on whether the question has a question word (like "who" or "what").

1. Questions with Question Words (Who, What, Where...)

Use the Plain Form of the verb/adjective followed by か.

  • 何なにを食たべるか、決きめていません。
    (I haven't decided what I will eat.)
  • いつ日本にほんへ行いくか、教おしえてください。
    (Please tell me when you will go to Japan.)

2. Yes/No Questions: ～かどうか

When there is no question word (e.g., "whether or not"), use ～かどうか.

  • おいしいかどうか、わかりません。
    (I don't know whether or not it is delicious.)
  • 明日あした暇ひまかどうか、聞きいてみます。
    (I will try asking whether or not [he] is free tomorrow.)

3. Sentence Structure

Type Connection
Verbs / い-Adj Plain Form + か / かどうか
な-Adj / Nouns Noun (no da) + か / かどうか

4. Common Verbs used with this structure

This pattern is almost always followed by verbs of knowing, checking, or deciding:

  • わかる (to know/understand)
  • しる (to know)
  • しらべる (to check/investigate)
  • きめる (to decide)
  • おぼえる (to remember)
