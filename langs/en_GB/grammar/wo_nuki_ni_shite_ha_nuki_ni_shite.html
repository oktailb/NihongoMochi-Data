Without...: ～を抜きにして（は）

The expression `～を抜きにして（は）` (をぬきにして) is used to say that something cannot happen or exist if a certain element is removed. It translates to "without...", "leaving out...", or "if we exclude..."

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `を抜きにして（は）` directly to a `Noun`.

`[Noun] + を抜きにしては + [Negative Result]`

  • かれの協力きょうりょく`を抜きにしては`、この計画けいかくは成功せいこうしません。
    (`Without` his cooperation, this plan cannot succeed.)
  • お世辞せじ`を抜きにして`、あなたの日本語にほんごは本当に上手です。
    (`Leaving out` flattery [honestly], your Japanese is really good.)
  • 冗談じょうだん`を抜きにして`、まじめに話し合いましょう。
    (`Leaving out` the jokes, let's talk seriously.)

2. Key Nuance: Indispensable Element

When followed by `～は` and a negative verb, it emphasizes that the noun is absolutely essential. Without it, the following action or state is impossible.

3. Formal vs Casual

In casual speech, you might hear `～抜きで` (ぬきで) used in a similar way (e.g., わさび抜きで - without wasabi).
