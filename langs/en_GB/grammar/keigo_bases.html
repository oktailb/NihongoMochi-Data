The Bases of Keigo (敬語けいご)

Keigo is the honorific system in Japanese. It is used to show respect, social distance, and politeness. In Japanese society, choosing the right level of Keigo depends on who you are talking to and your relative social standing.

1. The Three Types of Keigo

Keigo is divided into three main categories:

Category Japanese Name Usage
Polite 丁寧語ていねいご General politeness to anyone (uses desu/masu).
Honorific 尊敬語そんけいご To elevate the actions of a superior (boss, teacher, customer).
Humble 謙譲語けんじょうご To lower your own actions to show respect to a superior.

2. Respectful Form (尊敬語そんけいご) Patterns

This is used when talking about someone you respect. A common pattern is:

お + [Verb Stem] + になります

  • 先生せんせいはもうお帰かえりになりました。
    (The teacher has already returned home.)
  • チケットをお持もちになっていますか。
    (Do you [honored person] have your ticket?)

3. Humble Form (謙譲語けんじょうご) Patterns

This is used when talking about your own actions directed toward a superior. A common pattern is:

お + [Verb Stem] + します

  • お荷物にもつをお持もちします。
    (I will carry your bags [humbly].)
  • 明日あした、お電話でんわします。
    (I will call you tomorrow.)

4. When to use Keigo?

Think of it as an "In-group/Out-group" system (Uchi-Soto). You use Keigo for people outside your circle (customers, strangers) or people above you in rank (your boss). You do not use it for yourself or your own family members when talking to others.
