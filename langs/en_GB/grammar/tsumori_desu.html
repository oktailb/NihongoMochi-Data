Expressing Intention: ～つもりです

To talk about your plans or intentions for the future, use the structure `～つもりです`. It expresses a decision that you have already made.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `つもりです` to the `Dictionary Form` of a verb for affirmative intentions, or to the `Nai-form` for negative intentions.

`[Verb Plain Form] + つもりです`

Affirmative (Plan to do):

  • 来年らいねん、日本にほんに行いく`つもりです`。
    (I plan to go to Japan next year.)
  • 新しいあたら車くるまを買かう`つもりです`。
    (I intend to buy a new car.)

Negative (Plan not to do):

  • お酒さけを飲のまない`つもりです`。
    (I intend not to drink alcohol.)
  • 明日あしたはどこへも行いかない`つもりです`。
    (I plan not to go anywhere tomorrow.)

2. Difference between ～つもり and ～ようと思っている

While both express intention:

  • `～つもりです` sounds more certain and reflects a firm decision.
  • `～ようと思っている` (Volitional + と思っている) sounds more like a floating idea or something you are currently considering.

3. Important Note

You should generally not use `つもりです` to talk about the plans of a superior or someone you don't know well, as it can sound a bit presumptuous. It is best used for yourself or close friends.
