Naming and Content: ～という

This is one of the most versatile phrases in Japanese! `～という` (written as `～という`) is used to give names to things, define content, or report what someone else said. It's like a linguistic "equal sign" between a label and the thing itself.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun A` + という + `Noun B` (Noun B called A)
  • `Sentence` + という + `Noun` (The fact/news that...)

2. Essential Examples

  • これは何なに`という`花はなですか？
    (What is this flower called?)
  • 田中たなかさん`という`人ひとから電話でんわがありました。
    (There was a phone call from a person called Tanaka-san.)
  • 彼かれが結婚けっこんした`という`噂うわさを聞きいた。
    (I heard a rumor that he got married.)

3. Sensei's Note

Think of `～という` as a bridge. It connects a name (Tanaka) to a category (person), or a specific detail (he got married) to a general concept (rumor). It's the most common way to introduce new information or specify exactly what you are talking about!
