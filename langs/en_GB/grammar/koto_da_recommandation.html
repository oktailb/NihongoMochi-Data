Direct Advice: ～ことだ

When you want to give someone a piece of direct advice, like a teacher to a student or a senior to a junior, we use ～ことだ. It means "The best thing to do is..." or "You should..." It focuses on what is most important in a situation.

1. Sentence Structure

  • Verb (Dictionary Form) + ことだ
  • Verb (Nai Form) + ことだ

2. Wise Examples

  • 日本語にほんごが上手じょうずになりたければ、毎日話はなすことだ。
    (If you want to become good at Japanese, you should speak it every day.)
  • 健康けんこうのために、あまりお酒さけを飲のまないことだ。
    (For your health, the best thing is not to drink too much alcohol.)
  • わからないことは、すぐに先生せんせいに聞きくことだ。
    (When you don't understand something, you should ask the teacher immediately.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a very "instructional" way to give advice. It's often used when there is one clear, logical step to solve a problem. It's stronger than ~hou ga ii and sounds a bit more authoritative. Use it when you are certain about the best path forward!
