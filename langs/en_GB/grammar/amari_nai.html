Not very / Not much: あまり～ない

The adverb `あまり` (often written in hiragana) is used with a `negative verb or adjective` to mean "not very" or "not much." It softens a negation by indicating that while something exists, its degree is low.

1. Sentence Structure

Place `あまり` before the verb or adjective, and make sure the ending is `negative`.

  • この料理りょうりは`あまり辛からくない`です。
    (This food is `not very` spicy.)
  • 最近さいきん、`あまり忙いそがしくありません`。
    (Lately, I'm `not very` busy.)
  • テレビを`あまり見みません`。
    (I `don't` watch TV `much`.)

2. Nuance

Using `あまり` sounds more polite and indirect than a flat negation. For example, saying "It's not very delicious" (`あまり　おいしくない`) is gentler than saying "It's not delicious" (`おいしくない`).

3. Formal vs Casual

In casual speech, `あまり` is sometimes pronounced as `あんまり`.

  • これ、`あんまり`よくないね。
    (This one isn't very good, is it?)
