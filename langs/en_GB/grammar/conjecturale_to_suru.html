Attempting an Action: ～(よう)とする

This pattern is used to describe an attempt to do something, often right at the moment the action is about to start, or an effort that might not succeed. It's like saying "trying to" or "about to."

1. Sentence Structure

`[Verb Volitional Form] + とする`

2. Action-Packed Examples

  • 家いえを出で`ようとした`とき、雨あめが降ふり出だした。
    (Just as I was about to leave the house, it started to rain.)
  • 彼かれは何なにか言い`おうとしていた`。
    (He was trying to say something.)
  • 窓まどを開あけ`ようとした`が、しまっていた。
    (I tried to open the window, but it was locked.)

3. Sensei's Note

When used in the negative `～ようとしない`, it expresses that someone has no intention of doing something or refuses to do it. For example: `彼は何も食べようとしない` (He won't try to eat anything). It's a strong way to describe someone's stubbornness!
