Not Easily: なかなか～ない

Sometimes things just don't happen as quickly or as easily as we want. When `なかなか` is paired with a negative verb, it means "not easily," "not quite," or "taking longer than expected." It expresses a sense of frustration or a persistent state.

1. Sentence Structure

`なかなか` + Verb (Negative Form)

2. Patient Examples

  • 漢字かんじが`なかなか覚おぼえられない`。
    (I can't easily memorize Kanji / It's taking a long time to learn.)
  • バスが`なかなか来きない`。
    (The bus just isn't coming [and I've been waiting].)
  • この問題もんだいは難むずかしくて、`なかなか解とけない`。
    (This problem is difficult and I just can't solve it [despite trying].)

3. Sensei's Note

Be careful! On its own, `なかなか` means "quite" or "fairly" (like `なかなか　いい　ね！` - That's quite good!). But when you add that `~ない`, the meaning flips to "not easily." It's one of the most useful words for expressing the struggles of daily life!
