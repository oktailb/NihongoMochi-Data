Formal Contexts: ～において / ～における

In the world of formal Japanese, we don't just use `で` or `に` for locations and times. `～において` is the sophisticated way to say "in," "at," or "as for." It defines the stage or context where something happens.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun` + において (Used as an adverb)
  • `Noun` + における + `Noun` (Used as an adjective)

2. Professional Examples

  • 現代げんだい`において`、情報じょうほうは非常に大切だ。
    (In the modern world, information is extremely important.)
  • 東京とうきょう`において`、オリンピックが開催かいさいされた。
    (The Olympics were held in Tokyo.)
  • 人生じんせい`における`成功せいこうとは何だろうか。
    (What is "success" in life?)

3. Sensei's Note

Think of this as the difference between "I'm at the park" and "In the domain of public parks." `～において` sounds much more academic, official, and broad. You'll see it constantly in newspapers and academic papers!
