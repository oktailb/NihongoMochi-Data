Possibility and Impossibility: ～うる / ～えない

Life is full of possibilities, but also hard limits! To express that something is "possible to happen" or "impossible to happen" based on circumstances or the nature of things, we use `～うる` (or `～える`) and `～えない`.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach these to the `Verb Stem`.

  • `Possible:` [Verb Stem] + `うる / える`
  • `Impossible:` [Verb Stem] + `えない`

2. Memorable Examples

  • それは十分じゅうぶんにあり`うる`話はなしだ。
    (That is a story that could very well happen / a plausible scenario.)
  • そんなことはあり`えない`！
    (That's impossible! / No way that can happen!)
  • この計画けいかくには、失敗しっぱいし`うる`ポイントがいくつかある。
    (In this plan, there are several points where things could possibly fail.)

3. Sensei's Note

Pay attention to the reading! While it is often written as `うる`, in its dictionary form it can be read as `うる` or `える`. However, when you conjugate it (negative, past, etc.), it usually follows the `える` pattern (e.g., `えない`, `えた`). This is for "logical" possibility, not "personal ability" (for that, use the standard potential form like `たべられる`).
