Endurance and Completion: ～ぬく

In Japanese culture, persistence is everything! The suffix `～ぬく` (written as `～抜く`) expresses the idea of doing something with great effort until the very end, or doing it thoroughly despite difficulties.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `ぬく` to the `Verb Stem` (the `Masu-form` minus `~ます`).

`[Verb Stem] + ぬく`

2. Powerful Examples

  • 最後さいごまで走はしり`ぬいた`。
    (I ran all the way to the end / I finished the race against all odds.)
  • なやみ`ぬいて`、ようやく決心けっしんしました。
    (After agonizing over it thoroughly, I finally made a decision.)
  • どんなに辛つらくても、やり`ぬく`自信じしんがあります。
    (No matter how hard it gets, I have the confidence to see it through to the end.)

3. Sensei's Note

This isn't just "finishing." It's "finishing with `guts`!" It implies there was some hardship, struggle, or intense effort involved. If you just finished a sandwich, don't use `ぬく`. If you survived a 100-mile marathon, then `走りぬいた` is your word!
