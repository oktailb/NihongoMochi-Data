The Turning Point: ～を契機に (をけいきに)

Life is marked by moments that change everything. `～を契機に` (written as `～を契機に`) means "with X as a turning point" or "taking X as an opportunity." It's a formal way to describe a significant event that triggered a major shift or a new beginning.

1. Sentence Structure

`Noun` + を契機に / を契機として

2. Pivotal Examples

  • 病気びょうきを`契機に`、タバコを止やめた。
    (Taking my illness as a turning point, I quit smoking.)
  • 結婚けっこんを`契機に`、新あたらしい仕事しごとを始はじめた。
    (With my marriage as a catalyst, I started a new job.)
  • オリンピックを`契機として`、町まちが新あたらしくなった。
    (Taking the Olympics as an opportunity, the town was modernized.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is very similar to `~を　きっかけ　に`, but it's even more formal and "heavy." It suggests that the event was a major catalyst for a significant, often positive, change. It's used in history, business, and formal biographies to mark the "chapters" of a story!
