Giving to Me: くれる and くださる

In Japanese, `くれる` and `くださる` are special verbs for "to give." They are used `only` when someone gives something to `you` (the speaker) or to someone in your "inner circle" (like your family).

`The Direction:` Imagine an arrow pointing `toward you`.

1. The Two Levels of Giving to Me

Verb Politeness Usage
`くださる` Honorific (尊敬語そんけいご) A superior (boss, teacher, client) gives to you.
`くれる` Standard/Casual An equal or friend gives to you.

2. Basic Sentence Structure

The person who gives is the `subject` (marked with は or が).

`[Giver] は/が わたしに [Object] を くれる/くださる。`

  • 友達ともだちがプレゼントを`くれました`。
    (My friend gave me a present.)
  • 先生せんせいが本ほんを`くださいました`。
    (The teacher [honored person] gave me a book.)

3. Receiving a Favor: ～てくれる / ～てくださる

This is used when someone does something for you out of their own kindness. It emphasizes the giver's initiative.

  • 佐藤さとうさんが漢字かんじを教おしえて`くれました`。
    (Sato-san kindly taught me Kanji.)
  • 先生せんせいが直なおして`くださいました`。
    (The teacher kindly corrected [it] for me.)

4. Summary: The Final Rule of Thumb

  • `あげる (Away):` You give to them.
  • `もらう (Receive):` You receive from them (Focus on you).
  • `くれる (Toward):` They give to you (Focus on their kindness).

`💡 Grammar Note:` The command `ください` (Please) actually comes from `くださる`! When you say "Please give me...", you are literally saying "Kindly give [to me]."
