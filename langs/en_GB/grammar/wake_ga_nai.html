Impossible Logic: ～わけがない

When you want to say that something is absolutely impossible based on logic or common sense, we use `～わけがない`. It means "there is no reason that..." or "it is impossible that X." It is a very strong and confident denial.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Verb/Adj (Plain Form)` + わけがない
  • `Noun` + の / である + わけがない
  • `な-Adj` + な / である + わけがない

2. Resolute Examples

  • こんなに難むずかしい問題もんだい、子供こどもにできる`わけがない`。
    (There is no way a child can solve such a difficult problem.)
  • 彼かれが犯人はんにんな`わけがない`。昨日きのうは僕ぼくといたんだから。
    (It's impossible that he's the criminal. He was with me yesterday.)
  • あんなに練習れんしゅうしたんだから、負まける`わけがない`。
    (Since we practiced so much, there's no way we'll lose.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is much stronger than just saying `ない` (isn't). It implies that the very `reason` (wake) for something to happen is missing. It's the language of firm proof and unshakeable belief. In casual speech, it often becomes `～わけない`. Use it when you are 100% sure based on the facts!
