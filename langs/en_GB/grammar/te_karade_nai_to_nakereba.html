Prerequisites for Success: ～てからでないと / ～てからでなければ

Success is a journey of steps, and some steps cannot be skipped! `～てからでないと` means "unless X has happened first, Y cannot be done." It identifies a mandatory precursor or a required waiting period.

1. Sentence Structure

`[Verb Te-form] + からでないと / からでなければ` + [Negative Outcome]

2. Sequential Examples

  • 宿題しゅくだいを`やってからでないと`、遊びに行けません。
    (Unless you do your homework first, you can't go out to play.)
  • 手てを`洗あらってからでないと`、食べてはいけません。
    (You must not eat unless you wash your hands first.)
  • もっとよく`話はなし合ってからでなければ`、結論けつろんは出せません。
    (Unless we discuss it more thoroughly first, we cannot reach a conclusion.)

3. Sensei's Note

This is a very strict "logic gate." It's often used by parents and teachers to set rules, or in business to define necessary procedures. It tells the listener: "Don't even think about Y until you've finished X!"
