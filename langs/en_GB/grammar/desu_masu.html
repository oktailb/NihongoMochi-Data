Desu & Masu: The Armor of Politeness (丁寧語ていねいご)

In Japan, politeness isn't optional—it's a martial art. To navigate the streets of Shibuya or order ramen without looking like a barbarian, you need your two best allies: `Desu` and `Masu`.

"Polite style (ていねいご) is like the icing on a cake: it makes everything look more presentable."

1. です: The Verb "To Be" (Sort of)

`Desu` is placed at the end of a sentence to state something politely. It usually follows a noun or an adjective.

  • 学生がくせいです。 (I am a student.)
  • 可愛かわいいです！ (It's cute!) - `Essential for 99% of situations in Japan.`

2. ます: The Verbs' Glow-up

While `Desu` handles nouns, `Masu` is the bodyguard for verbs. It transforms a raw action into an elegant sentence.

  • 行いく (To go - Neutral) → 行いき`ます` (To go - Polite)
  • 食たべる (To eat - Neutral) → 食たべ`ます` (To eat - Polite)

3. The Negative Form "Power Up"

Need to say "no" with grace? Just change the ending. It's like switching from Super Saiyan mode to "Chilled" mode.

Type Affirmative (+) Negative (-)
Nouns / NA Adjectives ～です ～じゃありません
Verbs ～ます ～ません

4. Why use them?

Imagine you're in a `Samurai` anime. Using neutral form with a stranger is like unsheathing your sword for no reason. Using `です/ます` is like keeping your sword in its scabbard and bowing with respect.

`Golden Rule:` When in doubt, always use `です/ます`. It's your social shield!

`Pop Culture:` Even the strongest heroes like `Goku` sometimes use polite forms when speaking to deities. Be like a God, be polite.
