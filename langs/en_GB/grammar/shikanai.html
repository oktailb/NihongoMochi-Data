No Choice but to: ～しかない

The expression `～しかない` is used to express that there is no other option or alternative but to perform a certain action. It often conveys a sense of determination or resignation.

1. Sentence Structure

Attach `しかない` directly to the `Dictionary Form` of a verb.

`[Verb Dictionary Form] + しかない`

  • バスがありませんから、歩あるく`しかない`。
    (Since there are no buses, I have `no choice but to` walk.)
  • やる`しかない`！
    (I just have to do it! / There's `nothing for it but to` do it!)
  • 自分じぶんで直なおす`しかありません`。
    (There is `no other way but to` fix it myself.)

2. Difference between ～しかない and ～だけ

  • `～だけ:` Simply means "only" (neutral).
  • `～しかない:` Means "nothing but" and implies that the speaker is unsatisfied with the current situation or feels a lack of options.

3. Formal Version: ～ざるを得ない (N2)

In very formal or written Japanese, you might see `～ざるを得ない`, which carries a similar meaning of "having no choice but to...".
