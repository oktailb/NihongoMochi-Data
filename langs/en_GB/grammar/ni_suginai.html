Nothing More Than: ～にすぎない

Sometimes we need to put things in perspective. `～にすぎない` (written as `～に過ぎない`) is used to say that something is "nothing more than" or "merely" X. It's a way of de-emphasizing importance or scale.

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun` + にすぎない
  • `Verb (Plain Form)` + にすぎない

2. Humble Examples

  • それはただの噂うわさ`にすぎない`。
    (That is nothing more than a rumor.)
  • 私わたしは自分じぶんの仕事しごとをした`にすぎません`。
    (I merely did my job.)
  • これはまだ計画けいかくの一環いっかん`にすぎない`。
    (This is just one part of the plan.)

3. Sensei's Note

The verb `すぎつ` means "to exceed." So `に　すぎない` literally means "it does not exceed X." It's perfect for when you want to keep expectations realistic or when you want to sound modest about your achievements. Use it to keep things "grounded"!
