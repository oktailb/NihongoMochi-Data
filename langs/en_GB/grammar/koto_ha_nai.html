No Need To: ～ことはない

Sometimes we worry about things that aren't necessary. ～ことはない is used to tell someone (or yourself) that a particular action isn't required or that there's no need to go to such lengths.

1. Sentence Structure

Verb (Dictionary Form) + ことはない

2. Reassuring Examples

  • そんなに心配しんぱいすることはないよ。
    (There is no need to worry so much.)
  • 彼かれが来くるから、わざわざ電話でんわすることはない。
    (He is coming, so there's no need to go out of your way to call him.)
  • 君きみが謝あやまることはない。悪わるいのは彼かれだ。
    (You don't need to apologize. He is the one at fault.)

2. Sensei's Note

This is often used to give reassurance. It sounds kinder than a direct order. Use it when you want to relieve someone of a burden or a perceived obligation. It's the "don't sweat it" of Japanese grammar!
