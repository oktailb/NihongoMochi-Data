Negative Potential: "Cannot Do" (可能形かのうけい + ない)

So, you've learned how to say you "can" do something. But what if you're out of mana, or the boss is just too strong? To express that you "cannot" do something, we combine the `Potential Form` with the `Negative Form`.

"Just like a character with a status ailment, sometimes you just 能のうない (have no ability) to act!"

1. How to Conjugate

Once a verb is in its `Potential Form`, it automatically becomes a `Group 2 (Ru-verb)`. This makes turning it negative super easy: just swap the final る for ない.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

  • 行いく (To go) → 行いける (Can go) → 行いけ`ない` (Cannot go)
  • 話はなす (To speak) → 話はなせる (Can speak) → 話はなせ`ない` (Cannot speak)
  • 泳およぐ (To swim) → 泳およげる (Can swim) → 泳およげ`ない` (Cannot swim)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

  • 食たべる (To eat) → 食たべられる (Can eat) → 食たべられ`ない` (Cannot eat)
  • 見みる (To see) → 見みられる (Can see) → 見みられ`ない` (Cannot see)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する (To do) → できる (Can do) → `できない` (Cannot do)
  • 来くる (To come) → 来こられる (Can come) → 来こられ`ない` (Cannot come)

2. The Particle Rule

Even in the negative, the potential form still prefers the particle が over を to mark the thing you can't do.

  • 漢字かんじが読よめない。 (I cannot read Kanji.)
  • 納豆なっとうが食たべられない。 (I cannot eat Natto.)

3. Social Buff: Politeness

In a professional setting or when talking to your `Senpai`, don't forget to add です after ない or use the ません ending.

Style Ending Example
Casual ～ない 行いけない
Polite ～ないです / ～ません 行いけません

`Pro Tip:` Using できない is a very common way to say you're busy or unable to help without being too blunt. It's the ultimate "out of mana" excuse!
