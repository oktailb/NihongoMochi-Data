Resemblance: ～みたい

The expression `～みたい` is used to describe something that looks like or behaves like something else. It is the casual version of `～のようです` and is extremely common in daily conversation.

1. Visual Resemblance (Looks like)

Use it when you make a guess based on what you see, or when you want to compare two things.

  • あの雲くもはアイスクリーム`みたい`です。
    (That cloud looks like ice cream.)
  • かれは子供こども`みたい`に笑わらいます。
    (He laughs like a child.)

2. Conjecture (It seems that...)

It can also be used to express a guess about a situation.

  • 雨あめが降ふった`みたい`です。
    (It seems that it rained [the ground is wet].)
  • 田中たなかさんはもう帰かえった`みたい`ですよ。
    (It seems that Mr. Tanaka has already gone home.)

3. Connection

It attaches directly to `Nouns` and the `Plain Form` of verbs and adjectives.

Type Connection
Verbs / い-Adj Plain Form + みたい
な-Adj / Nouns Word (no `だ`) + みたい

4. Summary: mitai vs rashii

  • `～みたい:` Focuses on visual appearance or direct comparison (It looks like X).
  • `～らしい:` Focuses on hearsay or typical qualities (I heard X / It's typical of X).
