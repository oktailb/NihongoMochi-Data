The Verb Stem (連用形れんようけい)

The `Verb Stem` (also called the `Masu-stem` or `Base 2`) is the most fundamental building block in Japanese conjugation. Once you know the stem, you can attach dozens of different endings to change the meaning of the verb.

1. How to form the Stem

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final `"u"` sound of the dictionary form to the corresponding `"i"` sound.

  • 買か`う` → 買か`い`
  • 聞き`く` → 聞き`き`
  • 話はな`す` → 話はな`し`
  • 遊あそ`ぶ` → 遊あそ`び`

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Simply remove the final `る`.

  • 食たべ`る` → 食た`べ`
  • 見み`る` → 見み
  • 寝ね`る` → 寝ね

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

Memorize these two:

  • する → `し`
  • 来くる (kuru) → `来き` (ki)

2. Why is the Stem important?

The stem is used to create many common forms. Here are a few examples you will learn:

  • `Stem + ます:` Polite form (行いきます)
  • `Stem + たい:` Expressing desire (行いきたい)
  • `Stem + ながら:` Simultaneous actions (歩あるきながら)
  • `Stem + すぎる:` Doing too much (食たべすぎる)
  • `Stem + なさい:` Giving an order (飲のみなさい)

3. Formal Usage

In formal writing, the stem can be used by itself to connect two sentences, much like the `Te-form`.
