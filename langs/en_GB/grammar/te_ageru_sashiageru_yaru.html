Giving: あげる, さしあげる, and やる

In Japanese, choosing the right word for "to give" depends entirely on the social relationship between the giver and the receiver. These verbs are used when `you give to someone else`, or when `someone gives to someone else`.

`The Golden Rule:` These verbs are NEVER used when someone gives something `to you`. (Use `くれる` for that!)

1. The Three Levels of Giving

Verb Politeness Usage
`さしあげる` Honorific To a superior (boss, teacher, client).
`あげる` Polite/Standard To equals, friends, or family.
`やる` Casual/Downward To subordinates, children, pets, or plants.

2. Basic Examples

  • 私わたしは友達ともだちにプレゼントを`あげました`。
    (I gave a present to my friend.)
  • 先生せんせいに本ほんを`さしあげました`。
    (I [humbly] gave a book to my teacher.)
  • 犬いぬにエサを`やりました`。
    (I gave food to the dog.)

3. Giving a Favor: ～てあげる

When you do an `action` as a favor for someone, use the `Te-form + あげる`.

  • 荷物にもつを持もって`あげます`。
    (I will carry your bags for you.)
  • 友達ともだちに英語えいごを教おしえて`あげました`。
    (I taught English to my friend [as a favor].)

`⚠️ Pédagogical Warning:` Be careful! Saying "～てあげる" to a superior or even a stranger can sound a bit patronizing (like you are pointing out that you are doing them a favor). In those cases, it's more polite to use `～ましょうか` (Shall I...?).

4. Visualizing the Direction

Imagine an arrow pointing `away` from you:

`[Giver] は [Receiver] に [Object] を あげる。`

The giver is the topic (は), and the receiver is the target (に).
