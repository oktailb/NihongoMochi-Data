Causative-Te: "Let me do..." (使役しえき + て形てけい)

In the world of Shonen manga, you often hear heroes shouting for a chance to fight or asking for permission. This is the realm of the `Causative-Te` form. It's how you say "Please let me do [something]" or "Allow me to [something]".

"Think of it as the ultimate request buff. You're asking for the green light to act!"

1. The Recipe

To create this form, you first turn the verb into its Causative Form (せる / させる), then convert that to the Te-form. Since causative verbs always end in る (Group 2), they always end in せて or させて.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

  • 行いく (To go) → 行いかせる → 行いかせて
  • 聞きく (To listen) → 聞きかせる → 聞きかせて
  • 言いう (To say) → 言いわせる → 言いわせて

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

  • 食たべる (To eat) → 食たべさせる → 食たべさせて
  • 見みる (To see) → 見みさせる → 見みさせて

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する (To do) → させる → させて
  • 来くる (To come) → 来こさせる → 来こさせて

2. Common Combos

We almost never use the `Causative て` alone. We pair it with auxiliary verbs to specify the level of politeness and request.

Level 1: The Request (+ ください)

The standard way to say "Please let me..."

  • 私わたしに話はなさせてください。 (Please let me speak.)

Level 2: The Humble Favor (+ いただけますか)

Very polite, often used with bosses or strangers.

  • 明日あした、休やすませていただけますか？ (Could I please take tomorrow off?)

3. Social Context

Using this form is like putting your character in a "Humble" stance. You are acknowledging that the other person has the power to allow your action.

Pop Culture Check: In the famous song "First Love" by Utada Hikaru, you might hear 言いわせてください—"Please let me say [it]". It's a powerful way to express a deep desire to act.
