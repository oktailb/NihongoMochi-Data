Wishing for the Impossible: ～ものなら

We all have dreams that seem out of reach. `～ものなら` is used to express a wish for something that is actually very difficult or impossible to achieve. It's like saying "If only I could..." followed by a deep desire.

1. Sentence Structure

`[Verb Potential Form] + ものなら`

2. Dreamy Examples

  • やり直なおせる`ものなら`、もう一度人生をやり直したい。
    (If only I could start over, I'd want to live my life again.)
  • 帰かえれる`ものなら`、すぐにでも国くにへ帰かえりたい。
    (If it were possible to go back, I'd want to return to my country right now.)
  • あの日ひに戻もどれる`ものなら`、あんなことは言わなかったのに。
    (If only I could go back to that day, I wouldn't have said such a thing...)

3. Sensei's Note

This pattern is for the heart's deepest, often impossible, wishes. It's much stronger and more emotional than a simple `~たら`. It acknowledges that the condition is unlikely to be met, making the wish feel all the more poignant. You can also use it to issue a challenge: `やれるものならやってみろ!` (If you think you can do it, then try it!).
