Leaving Out the Essentials: ～ぬきで / ～ぬきの

Sometimes you want something "without" a certain part. `～ぬきで` (written as `～抜きで`) means "without X" or "leaving out X." It can be used for food orders, or for metaphorical things like "skipping the small talk."

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Noun` + 抜きで / 抜きにして
  • `Noun` + 抜きの + `Noun`

2. Selective Examples

  • ワサビ`抜きで`お願いします。
    (Without wasabi, please.)
  • 冗談じょうだん`抜きで`話はなしましょう。
    (Let's talk without the jokes / Let's be serious.)
  • 彼かれ`抜きでは`、このプロジェクトは進すすまない。
    (Without him, this project won't move forward.)

3. Sensei's Note

Think of the verb `ぬく` (to pull out/remove). This expression is very practical. Whether you're at a sushi bar or a business meeting, it allows you to define exactly what should be removed from the equation. `～抜きにしては` is a stronger way to say "if we leave out X, then Y is impossible."
