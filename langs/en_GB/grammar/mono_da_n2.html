Social Norms and Nostalgia: ～ものだ / ～ものではない

In N2, `～ものだ` takes on more "weight." It is used to state a general truth, a social expectation, or a deep feeling of nostalgia. It's like saying "That's just how things are" or "One should..."

1. Sentence Structure

  • `Verb (Dictionary Form)` + ものだ (Social norm / Should)
  • `Verb (Ta-form)` + ものだ (Nostalgia / Used to)

2. Deep Examples

  • 目上めうえの人ひとには敬語けいごを使つかう`ものだ`。
    (One should use polite language with their superiors. - `Social norm`)
  • 昔むかしはよくこの公園こうえんで遊あそんだ`ものだ`。
    (I used to play in this park often in the old days. - `Nostalgia`)
  • お年寄としよりには親切しんせつにする`ものだ`。
    (One should be kind to the elderly.)

3. Sensei's Note

This isn't a rule like `~なければ　ならない`. Instead, it expresses common sense or a universal truth. When used with the past tense, it's like a warm sigh of memory. It shows you understand the deeper patterns of life and society!
